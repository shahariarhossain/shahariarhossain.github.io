Ongoing research · 2026–present · BSRM School of Engineering, BRAC University

Autonomous systems & swarm robotics

Research Assistant · Supervisor: Md Arif Billah, Ph.D., Assistant Professor, Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering

Overview

A group of simple mobile robots has to solve two problems at once: stay together as a coherent swarm, and make progress toward a goal in the environment. Social coordination on its own does not tell the group where to go, while each robot chasing its own local readings can pull the group apart. My research looks at how a swarm can do both with very little information: each robot senses only one local quantity from its surroundings, such as a concentration or a signal strength, and robots do not send each other messages.

Current work

I am extending a visual coordination approach inspired by how insects track small moving targets. In it, each robot reacts to the motion of a selected neighbour. On top of this social behaviour, every robot links changes in its own local reading to the turns it recently made, keeps that evidence in a short-term memory, and turns it into a steering cue. Because neighbours react to each other's motion, a change in one robot's heading can also influence the robots watching it, without any explicit communication. I am studying this idea in simulation with stationary, moving and multiple sources, comparing different memory designs and a randomised reference strategy, and preparing it for validation on physical robots.

A manuscript on this work is in preparation.

Methods & tools

State-space modeling and feedback control of swarm agents; nonlinear dynamics in MATLAB simulation; 3D robot design in Autodesk Fusion; a five-agent swarm platform on low-cost edge hardware; reinforcement learning and latent-state world models for adaptive trajectories and autonomous control.

Keywords

Swarm robotics; visual coordination; scalar fields; source seeking; temporal memory; motion-mediated interaction.
